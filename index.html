No catch, but there is a reason, and it's in your favor. The $295 is a founding partner rate, not the real price. I strictly limit my roster to 3 active partners at a time, and I'd rather prove the system on your market than pitch it in a slide deck. The pilot is Step 1 of the system, not a cheap version of it. You get one real Fusion Ad, built from your real proof, launched on real spend in your real market. If it proves itself, Step 2 scales it. If it doesn't impress you, you keep the ad and we part ways. The $295 is a filter: it keeps out owners who aren't serious, and it keeps me honest, because I only earn the partnership if the proof earns it first.

What exactly do I get for the $295?

One Fusion Ad, built for your niche and your market, from your job-site proof. It's in your hands in 7 days, or your $295 back. That guarantee covers delivery, and it's the only guarantee I make in writing at this stage (more on that below). The ad launches on real ad spend in your real market, and on Day 8 we review the numbers together. What you don't get: a template, a stock video with your logo pasted on it, or a free trial of management. The pilot is the product. The partnership comes after, and only if the numbers say it should.

What happens on Day 8? Am I locked into the $1,500 after that?

No. Day 8 is a Data and Scale Review: you and I look at what your ad actually did in your market, in plain numbers, and decide together. The partnership is month-to-month, with written 90-day expectations so both of us know exactly what "working" means. Expectations, not a contract term. You can still leave any month. You're never locked in, and the pilot ad is yours either way. One more thing, because everyone asks: the $295 isn't deducted from the retainer. It's carried, not credited. The winning ad itself rolls into the partnership. The fee doesn't come off the price. Here's the bridge most agencies never build: the pilot proves the ad, and the partnership scales the winner. You commit to Step 2 with data in hand, not faith. If the data says no, you walk away with a real ad for $295.

What does the $1,500 a month actually buy that the pilot doesn't?

The pilot proves one ad. The partnership builds your pipeline. It's $1,500 a month at the founding rate. Every month you get 3 new Fusion Ads, so your best hooks keep getting sharper instead of going stale. I run your full Meta campaigns, because the Fusion Ad is a feed asset. It creates demand where homeowners scroll instead of waiting for them to search. I keep testing: winners get more spend, losers get killed, and the learning compounds. I set up tracking that ties every booked estimate back to the ad that produced it, so you stop guessing what your money did. You get plain-English reporting on one number that matters: cost per booked estimate. And your pages get a full trust optimization pass, because homeowners check your Facebook before they call. The pilot is proof of concept. The partnership is the machine that turns proof into a pipeline.

I already ran ads and got zero clients. What actually went wrong?

Usually, nothing was wrong with your work. The sale was lost in the first 5 seconds. It's rarely because of poor craftsmanship or bad service. It's because the ads are boring: zero engaging hooks, weak scripting, and no clear call to action. In today's crowded digital world, attention is everything. If you can't capture it, you simply don't exist. Too many remodeling brands burn their hard-earned money on invisible ads, ending the month with wasted spend and sometimes ZERO new clients.

Why isn't a great video enough on its own?

Content is everything today, but one great video alone still isn't enough. It only converts as a system, and the system has three parts. The ad has to stop the scroll. Management has to put it in front of the right homeowners and kill what doesn't work. And the funnel has to finish the job: the homeowner clicks, checks your page and reviews, builds trust, and books. Break any one of the three and the deal dies. That's what a Fusion Ad fixes first. The pilot proves it, and the partnership runs all three.

How are you different from the agencies that already burned me?

The stories I hear are always the same: a monthly fee, a 12-page report nobody reads, settings "optimized," and leads that were already sold to 3 or 4 other contractors. Here's the difference, and it's structural. Most agencies never touch the ad itself. They run the same boring video everyone else runs and fiddle with the settings around it. I rebuild the Ad itself from your real proof: your crews, your jobs, your reviews. Your leads come only to you, from your own ad account, in your own market. And I take one contractor per niche, per market, so you never compete against another one of my clients. No shared leads. No hidden markup on your spend. No 12-month lock-in. No jargon report. Just an ad that looks like you, and numbers you can read.

Do you guarantee I'll get clients?

No. And you should run from anyone who does. Nobody honest can guarantee booked jobs, because homeowners, markets, and seasons all move. Here's what I do guarantee, in writing: your pilot ad in your hands in 7 days, or your $295 back. That's a delivery guarantee, and it's the honest one. The pilot exists for exactly this reason: instead of buying my promises, you buy proof. You watch your own ad perform in your own market for real money, and then you decide. In the partnership, we put 90-day expectations in writing before we scale, so "is this working" is never a feeling. It's a number we both agreed on.

I don't have professional video. My jobs aren't pretty on camera. Does this still work?

Good. Your phone is enough, and pretty is not the point. Homeowners don't hire the contractor with the glossiest video. They hire the one they believe. Real job-site clips, real photos, real reviews: that's the trust material, and it's the entire thesis of the Fusion Ad. The AI handles the hooks, the pacing, and the polish. Reality handles the trust. What I need from you is simple: clips from your jobs, photos of finished work, and your best reviews. Shot on a phone is perfectly fine. If your work is good, the camera already has everything it needs.

How much do I need to spend on ads? Is that on top of your fee?

Yes, and I'll say it plainly because agencies hide it: ad spend is fuel, not my fee. Your spend goes straight to Meta, inside your own ad account. I don't take a cut of it. If that range doesn't fit your budget, say so now, because the pilot can't prove anything on pocket change, and I'd rather turn you away than sell you a test designed to fail.

It's real, and it's the reason the work is good. I build every ad myself. No juniors, no outsourcing, no templates. There are only so many ads one person can build to this standard in a month. Scale kills quality, so seats stay scarce by design. Right now there are 3 founding seats. When they're gone, the founding rate closes with them.

I'm on job sites all day. How much of my time does this take?

Almost none, because the system was built for owners in trucks, not owners at desks. You fill out a 2-minute form. You send your proof from your phone. We talk once on Day 8. That's it. No weekly meetings, no homework, no 12-page reports to decode. The one job that stays yours is answering the phone, because booked estimates decay fast: the homeowner who just watched your ad and called is hottest in the first hour. If you won't answer the phone when booked estimates call, don't buy this. I build the pipeline. You close the jobs. That's the whole division of labor.

Yes. All of it. Your ad account is yours. Your Fusion Ads are yours. Your data is yours. The pilot ad is yours from day one, and everything built in the partnership stays with you if you ever walk away. No hostage clauses, no "approval" games, no deleting your ads on the way out.
